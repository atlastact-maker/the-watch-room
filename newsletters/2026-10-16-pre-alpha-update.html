Closed Pre-Alpha: what happens next

Opens Friday 30 October

Thank you to everyone who applied to join the closed Pre-Alpha of The Watch Room. Tester applications closed at the end of Thursday 15 October, and the review of applications begins today.

This update sets out the key dates, how accepted testers will access the simulator, the scenarios available at release and the systems included in this build.

Key dates

● Thursday 15 October

Tester applications closed.

● Friday 16 October

Review of tester applications begins. Successful applicants are emailed confirmation of their place on the tester list.

● Friday 30 October

Pre-Alpha release. Access opens to every accepted tester at the same time, and each is notified by email.

● Every Friday

Progress updates and development diaries, by email and on the Discord server.

● Throughout the Pre-Alpha

Further scenarios for each service, released individually once they have been reviewed and approved.

Accessing the Pre-Alpha

01 Confirmation of acceptance. Each application is reviewed individually, and successful applicants receive an email confirming their place on the tester list.
02 Access notification. Access opens to all accepted testers on Friday 30 October, and a second email confirms when the simulator is available.
03 Pre-Alpha briefing. Sign in on a laptop or desktop computer and read this briefing, which sets out the scenarios available and the scope of the current build.
04 Your first shift. Open the operator console and select Start shift, then choose the services under your command and the call volume. Calls begin once the resourcing page has been completed.

If you have not yet joined our Discord server, please join here so that you can be added to the private tester channel.

Opening scenarios

The Pre-Alpha will open with one scenario for each service, with further scenarios released over the following weeks. Each scenario varies between runs, including who is present at the address, where a fire is seated and how a patient responds, so we recommend completing each one more than once.

 

Fire and Rescue Service

House Fire, Persons Reported — Wythenshawe

A neighbour reports smoke and shouting from the adjoining property. Mobilise an appropriate attendance, commit crews in breathing apparatus, establish whether anyone remains inside and coordinate an ambulance for any casualty brought out.

Feedback requested: Whether the incident ground behaves realistically: BA, water supply and rescue timings, and whether the radio messages read as they would operationally.
 

Ambulance Service

Cardiac Arrest — Chorlton

A football coach reports that a player has collapsed during a match and is not breathing normally. Guide bystanders through CPR until the first resource arrives, then manage the patient through monitoring, the decision on critical care support and the pre-alert to hospital.

Feedback requested: Whether the patient responds realistically, and whether the vital signs change as a clinician would expect.
 

Police

Road Traffic Stop — Sale

A patrol has stopped a vehicle at the roadside. Carry out checks on the vehicle and driver, including PNC checks, a roadside breath test and a drug wipe, before deciding on the appropriate outcome.

Feedback requested: Whether the stop runs as it would at the roadside, and whether the checks and options are those an officer would expect, in the order they would expect them.

Systems in this build

999 call handling

Scripted callers who respond to the questions asked, opening codes (NICL, IRS and AMPDS), call grading, and pre-arrival advice delivered while resources are en route.

Mobilising and mapping

The county board with real stations and appliances, routing on the real road network, crews deployed on foot at the scene, and the mobile data terminal for managing each incident.

Fire ground control

Sectorisation, the BA board and entry control, water supplies from hydrants and relays, jets, tactical mode, and informative and stop messages.

Casualty care

A live vital signs monitor, triage sieve, airway and breathing management, oxygen and medication orders, packaging, hospital pre-alerts and conveyance.

Police ground control

Containment and cordons, arrest and search, PNC checks on people and vehicles, roadside breath and drug testing, domestic abuse risk assessment, custody and transport.

Debrief and service record

Every shift is assessed on mobilisation time, attendance, advice given and decisions made at the scene. The service record retains every shift, and Pre-Alpha testers keep their records into the wider release, with a tester mark against the callsign.

Tester responsibilities

Regular testing

Where possible, one or two shifts each week. A shift lasts between twenty minutes and an hour.

Fault reporting

Report faults through Help, then Report a problem, available on every screen. Each report carries the screen, the incident and the final minute of the incident log; add a short note of what you expected to happen.

Confidentiality

No footage, screenshots or reviews in public until public access has been announced. Discussion is welcome in the private tester channel on Discord.

Honest feedback

Where something does not reflect how the role is carried out in practice, that feedback is as valuable as any fault report.

Not included in this build

The full scenario library

A limited set of scenarios is available at release; the remainder are being reviewed individually.

Multiplayer

The current build supports a single operator.

Mobile devices

A laptop or desktop computer is required. Tablet views are intended for a second screen.

Final balancing

Timings and grading continue to be refined as the advisors report.

Not part of this round?

The Pre-Alpha has deliberately been kept small, and a wider test will follow. Your account remains registered and ready for the next phase. In the meantime, progress updates continue every Friday, and our Discord server remains the best place to follow development.
