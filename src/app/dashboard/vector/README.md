# VECTOR — the on-shift desk

The screen an operator sees on shift (`/dashboard`) is the VECTOR
command-and-control desk from `prototypes/vector-cad`, rebuilt as React
over the simulator that already lives in `dashboard-client.tsx`. Nothing
in here holds mock data: every row on the board is derived from the sim's
incidents, deployments, stations, ETAs and log, and every button calls a
simulator function that was already there.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `vector.css` | The design system: light and dark palettes, bars, tiles, cards, tables, screens, plus the overrides that bring the legacy panels' chrome in line. |
| `theme.ts` | Light / dark, remembered under `vector-theme` and stamped on `<html data-cad-theme>`. |
| `chrome.tsx` | Brand strip, menu bar, screen tabs, workspace bar, alerts strip, 999 banner, selected-incident strip, status bar. |
| `tile.tsx` | `VectorTile` — the movable, resizable panel — and the layout store (saved geometry, Overview / Resources presets, snap, Save layout / Restore saved). |
| `dispatch-tiles.tsx` | Calls, Live incidents, Incident details, Scene units, Attendance, Resources, County cover, Standby, Hospitals. |
| `log-tile.tsx` | The shift log as a tile, with the `LOG>` entry line. |
| `mdt-task-workspace.tsx`, `mdt-orders.ts`, `tasking-tile.tsx` | The prototype's task workspace (unit tasking, catalogue, numbered task order, task history, the water page) driving the simulator, with orders kept per incident. Opens from the Dispatch panel bar as **Unit tasking** for the unit in hand. |
| `patient-care.tsx` | Patient Care — one workspace for every casualty on an incident. Opens from the Dispatch panel bar as **Casualties** (the whole incident, for control) and from the MDT as **Patient care** (filtered to that resource's own patients). Either view pops out into its own window. |
| `fire-command.tsx`, `command-store.ts` | FIRE & RESCUE COMMAND — the MDT's Fire module: roles strip with the tactical mode (offensive · defensive · transitional, declared to the sim), the fire picture (stage, growth or knock-down, flashover countdown, exposure, jets and BA at work), incident summary with the scene plan, assessment, command plan with objectives and a review clock, sectors with a commander, a task and the appliances working each, crew tasking by function, appliances and roles, assistance messages to control (make pumps N, ambulance, police, aerial, water carrier, hazmat, command unit, utilities), water supply, BA entry control and the log, over Overview · Assessment · Sectors · Crews · Appliances · Water · BA control · Log tabs. The commander's record lives in the store per incident and goes to the shift log when recorded. |
| `scene/model-sprites.ts`, `scene/model-sprite.tsx` | GENERATED by `scripts/render-model-sprites.mjs` from `assets/models/*.glb`: each 3D model (a Meshy export) rendered from directly above under the scenes' light into `public/models/<key>.png`, with its size in metres, so a scene lays it in its own metre space; a model with an animation clip becomes a sheet of frames with its frame rate. `ModelSprite` draws one and plays a sheet (one frame through a clip, the sheet stepping under it). The hydraulic spreaders and the firefighter on the RTC stage are the first: the crew run in from the appliance to their first job, walk from one job to the next round the wreck, and walk off when a job ends with nothing to follow; `assets/models/README.md` says what to export and how to add one. |
| `scene/ground-views.ts`, `scene/ground-rtc.tsx`, `scene/plan-vehicles.tsx`, `rtc-state.ts` | The tablet's Incident app has three pages — **Job** (the facts and the log), **Map** (the scene plan) and **Ground**, the whole scene drawn from above, offered only for the scenarios listed in `ground-views.ts` (the M60 RTC first; ground views are built job by job, and the simpler jobs may never get one). It turns to Ground on its own when the unit books in attendance. The RTC ground view reads the same deployments, tasks, log and sim state as every other screen and stores nothing: the carriageway from the scene with the traffic still running in whichever lanes are live (both ways, slowed past the work, queued behind a rolling block); the wrecks as the stage draws each one (`RtcWreck`, the stage's picture without its frame), turned the way they came to rest; the appliances where the operator parked them on the map (the ground map's own sprite art, an ambulance drawn here, a car in the service's colour for the rest), or where a fend-off, a rolling block or a closure at the junction has put them, sliding there as the task runs; the crews running from their appliance to a job and walking back, standing by the appliance between jobs, the officers along a cone taper as it goes out; the ambulance crews with the casualties in the cars and the walking wounded on the hard shoulder; the witness's car and the caller's lorry; the overbridge; night with the scene lighting pooling on the road, and rain. Wide · Scene · Wreck framings; tapping a unit picks it on the tablet, and that is the only control — everything is done from the modules' own pages. `rtc-state.ts` is the reading of the tasks (done, working, who came from where) the stage and the ground view share. |
| `rtc-stage.tsx`, `scene/plan-rtc.tsx` | The Fire module's RTC page as a stage, laid out like casualty care: the crashed vehicle fills the screen from above as a cutaway (the person in the driver's seat, the shell changing as blocks go in, glass comes out, doors and roof come off and the dash is rolled, the firefighters at whichever job is running, the ambulance crew with the casualty), the cards floating over it (the track from scene to handover, the headline, the release clock against the twenty-minute target, the vehicles, facts, live hazards, the record), and a round bar along the foot — Command · Vehicle · Make safe · Glass · Space · Extricate · Hazards · Casualties · Log — each opening a section over the picture. Every job is the same `crs_action` or `rtc_extrication` task. **Road** is the live carriageway (`src/lib/sim/traffic.ts`): the lanes as a strip over the picture (cars running past, cones out, the road held), the headline and what is in force, this appliance's fend-off, and the asks to control for police and National Highways; the stage alerts while anything runs beside the work. |
| `police-controls.tsx`, `police-store.ts` | POLICE CONTROLS — the MDT's police module: incident summary, the unit and the person or vehicle in front of it, the scene map, and Resource actions over General · Traffic · People · Vehicles tabs (details, accounts, stop and search, arrest, welfare, vehicle search with reason, grounds and power, closures placed on the map, traffic held and released, scene equipment, support requests), and on a job with a live carriageway the lanes themselves: a fend-off, lanes coned off at the scene, a rolling road block from the upstream junction and the carriageway closed there with a diversion (the block and the closure are roads policing work), each a task that stays in force until stood down, Current activity and the log. Every action is a simulator task on the crew; the people and vehicles are the job's records, "Person 01" until details are taken and confirmed on the PNC. The officer's own record (accounts, welfare, searches, traffic controls, support, the firearms declaration) lives in the store. |
| `pnc-page.tsx` | PNC on the tablet — the enquiry form (Vehicle · Names · Property, reason, incident, unit), recent enquiries and the terminal's block-capital return with Summary / Report details and result paging. Runs the same LEDS enquiries as the desk and writes to the same audit. |
| `subject-tile.tsx` | SUBJECT VEHICLE — the desk's view of the car a job is chasing (engine in `src/lib/sim/subject.ts`, map layer in `components/subject-layer.tsx`): the breadcrumb of camera reads and direction of travel, the live track once a crew has eyes on it, units sent to hold a camera site or junction ahead, area search, and the tactics — stop, follow, TPAC box, stinger, contact — once the track is live. |
| `anpr-page.tsx` | ANPR on the tablet — the car's own camera or a fixed site, a camera view with the plate, live reads with filters, a VRM search over the captured reads, and the selected read's match review (three checks, PNC enquiry, source report, confirm or dismiss). Deterministic traffic, the job's own vehicles slipped in. |
| `vital-monitor.tsx` | The vital signs monitor as one component with its own record per patient — traces, numbers, the NIBP cuff, the 12-lead and the alarm limits — pinned in the tablet's top strip beside the unit selector and used in the casualty care screen's monitor card. |
| `casualty-care.tsx` | CASUALTY CARE — the per-patient screen opened from Patient Care: patient assessment with the body figure and primary survey, the vital signs monitor with sweeping ECG / pleth / resp traces, oxygen and medication, the treatment log, and the Assess · Airway · Breathing · Circulation · Immobilise · Handover bar. Every control calls the simulator's treatment handlers. |
| `call-screen.tsx` | The 999 Call screen: caller strip, location fixes, confirm address, scripted assessment and dialogue, incident type, other services, ready-to-open checklist, CREATE INCIDENT & MOBILISE. |
| `mob-screen.tsx` | The Mobilising screen: attendance slots, resource cards, mobilising message, turnout times, station bays, standby cover. |
| `desk-model.ts` | `useDeskModel` — the one place the tiles' and screens' rows are derived from simulator state. |
| `desk.tsx` | `VectorDesk` — composes the shell, the four screens and the Dispatch workspace around the simulator's map and its existing panels. |

Screens: **Dispatch** (map-first workspace with tiles), **999 Call**,
**Mobilising**, **Ground** (the ground map with the assigned-units strip
for selecting, placing and turning units, and the rugged MDT — now a
patient-care terminal that shows only the CASUALTY CARE screen, on the
tablet or in its own window). F2–F5 switch between them.

The legacy panels (classic call stack, dispatch log, incident card,
resources list, LEDS, ANPR, search, 999 call log, vehicle and pre-arrival
sheets, station bays) still exist and are reachable from the Comms,
Resources and Systems menus; `cad-theme.ts` points their design tokens at
the VECTOR palette so they follow the light / dark switch.
